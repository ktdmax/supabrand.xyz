## Editorial rhythm
Instagram can hold one small observation: a carrier packed for the market, a mechanic's hands, the pause before a commute. LinkedIn explains organisational decisions and operator experience. TikTok should begin with something actually happening, not a spinning logo. YouTube can spend more time on a useful demonstration. These are editorial choices; there is no minimum number of smiling people, cuts, colour blocks or sustainability references.

## Formats
Plan 4:5 feed artwork and 9:16 vertical stories/video; check live platform requirements before publishing. Protect meaningful content from interface overlays using the current publishing team's preview. On Instagram a 10% inset is a studio starting point, not a universal platform safe-area guarantee. Put subtitles on spoken video and meaningful text alternatives on image posts. Avoid flashing edits and cycling claims hidden only in captions.

## Channel boundaries
On LinkedIn, a group result stays a group result. Do not post it under a subbrand logo as if it describes that business alone. On TikTok, entertainment does not excuse a false battery, speed or emissions claim. On YouTube, attach the relevant test qualification to the spoken or on-screen performance claim, not only to an external document. Press approval is required for embargoed launches even when a creator received a sample.

Partnership footage needs both the territory and the platform permission. A global profile's geographic reach matters: a Europe-only grant is not made global by writing the caption in English. Paid amplification is a separate placement from an organic post. Templates supplied with this desk pack are editable examples, not a clearance record.
