## A deck is a conversation
One idea per slide is a useful habit, not a rule that bans a comparison. Start with why the audience should care, then show the decision and supporting detail. Avoid a wall of slogans. Our normal office type is Arial, with the installed system sans as fallback; use live editable text for tables.

## Layout
Use the primary logo on the opening and closing slides. Section dividers may use the compact sign when the full name has already been established. A 12-column grid and 6% margins are a helpful default. Diagrams can fill more of a technical slide if marks and captions remain readable. Do not put a full partnership lockup on every footer.

## Evidence in decks
Every numerical product or corporate claim needs the relevant period and boundary on the slide or a clearly associated note visible to its audience. A confidential audience does not turn an unsupported statement into an approved fact. A finance chart labelled revenue share must not be presented as a count of products. Use source notes in speaker material in addition to audience-facing qualifications, not instead of them.

## Working examples
A wide photo with a small headline is one possible opening. A clear technical drawing may be better for an operator briefing. No rule requires a photograph, exactly seven words, or a particular number of slides. Partner permissions and font licences need checking separately from slide-master approval.
