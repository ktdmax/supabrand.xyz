# Movement with room to breathe


Motion Studio / 06 November 2025 / release 1.1
Approved by Digital Experience and Group Brand. For branded motion on social, YouTube, website and presentations; not a product operating specification.

## Rhythm
Movement begins with a reason: a useful detail appearing, a route opening, a sentence becoming clear. A gentle pace is often appropriate; there is no required number of seconds per shot. Do not use arbitrary speed ramps to imply faster product performance. Product actions should remain mechanically plausible.

## Identity animation
The two-track compact sign can reveal left to right; the final mark must match the released vector exactly. Do not bend the wordmark along a wheel. End-card visible minimums and clear space are those of the identity manual. A normal masterbrand end card uses the current signature. In-scope campaign placements use the campaign substitution; a YouTube film is not automatically in a paid-display campaign.

## Interface movement
Use 180–260 ms as a starting range for nonessential state transitions. Honour reduced-motion preferences: remove parallax, looping decorative travel and large position changes. Essential state changes remain perceivable without animation. Motion ranges are guidance; the reduced-motion behaviour is a delivery requirement for web implementations.

## Accessibility and qualifications
Subtitle spoken brand video. Provide a transcript for instructional YouTube material. Do not place a range-test condition on a single unreadable frame or make a disclaimer disappear before the claim. Review legibility in the final format; a fixed animation duration is not a guarantee of comprehension.

## Example storyboard
0–2 s: bicycle at rest, a bag placed on the rack.
2–5 s: detail of a useful fitting, one short sentence.
5–8 s: wide composition and brand end card.
This is an illustrative eight-second structure. A six-second asset with a clear useful idea can fit our guidance. The rack must actually belong to the pictured configuration. Music licences, performer releases and location permissions need their own records; brand approval covers none of them.
