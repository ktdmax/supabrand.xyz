# PICO / a smaller kind of big idea


PICO launch desk · 09.04.2026 · release 1.3
Approved: Consumer Product Council and Group Brand. Family: Glide. Model: SUPABRAND PICO G1.

## The proposition
An accessible electric scooter for the part of the day between places. PICO is curious, compact and practical. Its entry-level positioning describes price and specification, never the age or experience of a rider. Do not direct riding communication at children or suggest that an adult mobility product is a toy.

English product signature: **Small moves. Open possibilities.** Use this instead of the master signature in English PICO product heroes and product brochure covers. Optional on packaging and specification sheets. No local signature translations have been released; omit the line where a local signature would otherwise be needed. Consumer body copy can be written locally under the tone guide.

## Visual personality
Use Paper as the base, Signal as the focal field and rounded technical diagrams. Playfulness comes from rhythm and an observant sentence, not cartoon eyes on the product. Keep the endorsed PICO logo intact. Do not create a second compact mark for small social avatars; use the approved SUPABRAND compact sign with the profile name establishing PICO ownership.

## Market readiness
The July naming release controls sales availability. Earlier launch mood boards used London and Seoul street photographs as composition references; they were not territory approvals. No G1 consumer launch is released for either market. Global audiences may see a corporate portfolio description stating that PICO operates in selected markets, without a purchase invitation outside the release list.

## Product language
“Folds in three steps” refers to a stationary operating sequence. It does not mean three seconds or one-handed operation. Keep product imagery parked for folding demonstrations. Never imply road-use permission merely because a market has a commercial release. The social editorial tone is not safety clearance.

## Examples
“Pack up the last kilometre.” is a creative writing example. It is not a product dimension or a carrying-weight claim. The supplied social layout leaves room for the market and availability note; those fields must be populated for a live commercial placement.
